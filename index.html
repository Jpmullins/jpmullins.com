01 Research

Questions that guide the work.

I.

Artificial epistemology

When do belief, evidence, and revision become legitimate explanatory concepts for AI? I connect formal and naturalized epistemology with testable architectural conjectures.

Belief revision · Formal logic · Epistemic agency

II.

Agentic systems

How should agents maintain knowledge, use tools, and coordinate over time? My work explores modular agent architectures, evidence-grounded research, and multi-agent systems.

Agent architecture · Knowledge systems · Bounded autonomy

III.

Evaluation & assurance

What does an evaluation actually establish? I work on operational testing, adversarial evaluation, and model provenance, with attention to controls, evidence, and the limits of inference.

Operational evaluation · Model lineage · Adversarial testing

Public software and research.

01 / Independent

Aleph

A modular multi-agent harness with an evidence-grounded research suite. The knowledge layer treats claims and their support as first-class objects, so changes in evidence can propagate through a web of belief.

In development · Kernel and knowledge-layer redesign

Source
02 / Open-source collaboration

TorchSig

An open-source signal-processing and machine-learning toolkit. As lead developer from 2023 to 2025, I worked on dataset generation, transforms, spectrogram tools, training workflows, and GNU Radio integration.

RF machine learning · PyTorch · Signal processing

Source
03 / Independent

Holomoe

A research implementation of a whole-response latent predictor, combining a JEPA-style architecture, mixture-of-experts routing, and episodic memory. Executable synthetic worlds provide the correctness signal.

Experimental implementation · Not a validated architecture

Source

Institutional research · University of Maryland

AI assurance at ARLIS

At the Applied Research Laboratory for Intelligence and Security, I lead data science and engineering work on AI evaluation, model provenance, and operational testing, and contribute to adversarial evaluation of language models and agentic systems.

This is ARLIS research, distinct from my independent projects. Institutional systems and restricted material are not reproduced here.

04 About

Philosophy,
with an engineering practice.

I am a Visiting Associate Research Scientist at ARLIS, University of Maryland, and a Research Fellow in AI/ML and NLP at the University of Kansas’s Center for Cyber-Social Dynamics.

My background spans formal logic, philosophy of science, RF machine learning, and AI evaluation. Earlier work in the U.S. Intelligence Community informs my interest in how systems behave under realistic operational conditions.

I earned my Ph.D. in Philosophy at the University of Kansas in 2023. My dissertation, Quine’s Methodology: Science, Logic, and Ontology, examined the relationship between scientific inquiry, logic, and ontological commitment.
